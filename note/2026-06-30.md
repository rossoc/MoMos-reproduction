This seems a bit cucumbersome: L304-L324:

To address the bottleneck in Kolmogorov complexity upper-bound when the mosaic
term $\K(\M) \leq m \lceil \log_2 k \rceil$ dominates, we introduce V-Fold MoMos.
This method slices the $m$ blocks of the mosaic $\M$ into $m' = m / v$
composites, such that there are $v$ folds for each block in the composite.
Each fold is a subset of motifs $\Z$.
By restricting the choice of each block to a shorter specific fold,
V-Fold MoMos reduces the bit budget required to represent the mosaic entries.

In particular, we partition the block positions into $v$ folds of size 
$m' = m / v$.
Let $\Z \in \R^{s \times k}$ denote the global motif
dictionary.
Each fold $\I_j \in [v]$ corresponds to a mapping $[k'] \rightarrow [k]$,
therefore it represents $k'$ motifs selected from $\Z$.
This selection is defined by a fold mapping $\I_j \in [k]^{k'}$, such that $\Z_j
= \Z[\I_j]$, where 
$\Z[\I_j]$ denotes the column-wise indexing of $\Z$ by $\I_j$.

The size of the folds $k'$ is constrained by two bounds:
(1) the maximum: $k' \le k$, indeed we achieve a higher compression enforcing
smaller values of $k'$; (2) the size of each partition of $\M$: $k' \le \frac{m}{v} = m'$.
