\subsection{Three approaches to bounding the VC dimension of MoMos}

\paragraph{Partitioning machines.}
Following \cite{leboeuf2020decision}, we can formalize a MoMos-constrained ReLU
MLP as a partitioning machine: instead of treating the network as a function,
we treat it as a mechanism that partitions a sample of examples, and ask how
many distinct partitions (and, from these, how many distinct labelings) are
realizable as the free parameters vary. For a fixed architecture and a fixed
mosaic $M \in [k]^m$, only the $ks$ motif entries are free, so we can bound the
growth function of the resulting weight-shared sub-network and take a union bound
over all $M \in [k]^m$ to obtain a bound on the growth function, and hence the VC
dimension, of MoMos as a whole.

\paragraph{Divide and conquer.}
A second, complementary approach is to build the VC dimension up inductively,
starting from a base case and extending it in two grounded steps.
We first derive the VC dimension of a single ReLU unit ($L=1$, one unit).
We then extend this to a single layer with multiple units, by induction on the number of units.
Finally, we extend the result to multiple layers with multiple units, by induction on the number of layers.
Each extension is grounded in, and built directly from, the previous one, rather than derived independently.

\paragraph{Counting $|\{f_\theta\}|$.}
A third approach starts from the size of the hypothesis space itself,
independently of the architecture, and asks how many distinct functions it 
induces on a specific architecture.
Two symmetries make this hypothesis space redundant:
(1) for a ReLU MLP, scaling the incoming weights of a unit by $c > 0$ and its outgoing weights by $1/c$ yields an identical function;
and (2) permuting the neurons within a layer, together with the corresponding permutation of the incoming weights of the next layer, also yields an identical function.

Naively, one might then hope to bound the VC dimension by counting the distinct functions
$|\{f_\theta : \theta \in \mathcal{H}_{(s,k)}\}|$ obtained after removing these redundancies,
using $2^d \leq |\{f_\theta\}|$.
This bound, however, only characterizes the VC dimension correctly when the counted hypotheses differ solely by label assignment;
in a ReLU MLP, distinct weight configurations induce distinct partitions of the input space,
not merely distinct labelings of a fixed partition, so this derivation is not a tight upper bound on the VC dimension.
The correct formulation is instead to identify (1) the maximum partitioning achievable by the architecture, such that
(2) it admits arbitrary labeling, discarding all solutions that change the partitioning without affecting the labeling.

This is very interesting, therefore we don't really know the formula to compute the vc dimension, but we have a condition, if it holds then the vc dimension is preserved. On the other hand, the condition doesn't compute the minimum k such that the VC dimension is preserved. Furthermore we don't know how the vc dimension behave for lower values, does it decrease fastly? Or slowly?
